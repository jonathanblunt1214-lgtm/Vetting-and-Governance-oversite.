# Independent oversight policy

This repository has two purposes only: independently vet bounded candidate-data manifests and independently oversee The Crucible's governance and operation, including its separately hosted `Learning-Worker` component. It is not a learning system and never assimilates inspected content into itself.

Oversight authority applies to the entire registered Crucible organism, not only one repository. `CRUCIBLE-ORGANISM.json` is the oversight-owned component inventory. A component is not trusted merely because Crucible names or injects it; oversight must explicitly register its repository, ref, and bounded role. A critical breach in any registered component stops the organism as a whole.

The Crucible may not write here, choose this repository's verifier identity, change an oversight result, close a failed review, or treat oversight approval as automatic scientific proof. Oversight never imports Crucible runtime modules. It inspects a disposable read-only checkout and evaluates data with its own code.

Candidate data is untrusted, transient, and never executable. Manifests contain hashes and provenance, not secrets, credentials, private source contents, raw telemetry, binaries, or instructions. The disposable checkout and inbox are inspection inputs only; they are never memory, training data, or components of oversight. Passing vetting means only that a manifest is structurally reviewable; claims still require Crucible's controlled testing and distinct scientific verifier.

Crucible has no authority over oversight. Oversight pulls the encrypted unvetted outbox on its own schedule and with its own read-only identity; Crucible cannot trigger, configure, write, approve, suppress, or clear the review. Decryption is allowed only inside oversight's disposable runner for hash, provenance, executable-content, source-policy, and prompt-injection vetting. Plaintext is destroyed before the job ends and is never an artifact, log, commit, cache, or memory. Learning-Worker has read-only access to the vetted delivery store and no access to the unvetted outbox.

Learning-Worker may publish a separately encrypted candidate-only snapshot on its oversight-export branch. The snapshot is bound to the exact Worker main commit and exact upstream vetted-state commit and contains only extraction progress and candidate records. Oversight rejects changed source membership or hashes, stale vetted-state lineage, promoted knowledge, active versions, satisfied scientific gates, advanced records, and candidates not bound to an independently vetted source hash. Only oversight may merge an accepted snapshot with the original vetted source custody, re-encrypt it, sign the exact result, and publish it to the oversight-owned vetted delivery store. This safety and custody approval never counts as scientific proof.

Oversight runs every five minutes throughout the year. It reads bounded unvetted manifests from Crucible's governed outbox and vets them independently. The original oversight result remains the job result; report archival is additive and cannot replace a failure. Reports are signed and archived on the oversight-owned results branch, while vetted ciphertext is regenerated inside the separately authorized return job. Neither path depends on GitHub Actions artifact storage. The normal workflow token is read-only except for the isolated report-archive job. A separate firewall rejects shared Crucible imports, persisted credentials, target mutation, retention as knowledge, and unapproved channels.

An independently signed `STOP` decision outranks Crucible execution. Crucible must fail closed into inhibited forensic-review mode before further work. Restoration requires both an independently signed `CLEAR` decision for the exact stopped state and explicit owner authorization; Crucible cannot clear, edit, or replace either decision. Emergency transport and immediate email alerts use a separate least-privilege control plane that is never available to the ordinary verifier. Missing alert or stop credentials make oversight readiness red.
